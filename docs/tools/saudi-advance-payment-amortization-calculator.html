Complete Guide to Advance Payments, APG Guarantees, and Interim Payment Certificate (IPC) Deductions in Saudi Arabia

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, major infrastructure developments, Vision 2030 giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea Global, Diriyah Company, Qiddiya, ROSHN), and standard ministerial tenders governed by the Government Tenders and Procurement Law (GTPL) rely heavily on cash mobilization instruments. The Advance Payment mechanism provides contractors with working capital to mobilize site offices, procure long-lead plant and equipment, and secure baseline materials without incurring prohibitive early debt financing.

However, managing the financial recovery (amortization) of advance payments across Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs) requires rigorous accounting to prevent cash shortfalls, premature over-deductions, or delayed releases of Advance Payment Guarantees (APG). This comprehensive guide clarifies the statutory limits, deduction mechanisms, VAT interactions, and reconciliation practices required for commercial teams in KSA.

Saudi Etimad GTPL Standard Recovery Rule

Under standard Saudi public works contracts, advance payment is capped at 10% of the total contract value (or up to SAR 50,000,000 max under specific ministerial waivers). Deduction starts immediately on the first Interim Payment Certificate and is deducted pro-rata based on the percentage of total certified work performed:

Amortization Deduction = Current Gross IPC Work × Advance Rate %

Example: If Advance = 10% and Current Work = SAR 1,000,000, deduction is SAR 100,000 until the total cumulative SAR received in advance is fully liquidated.

FIDIC 1999 / 2017 Red & Yellow Book Clause 14.2

Many private developers and semi-governmental PIF portfolio projects adopt the FIDIC international standard. Rather than immediate deduction, amortisation commences only when cumulative certified work exceeds a milestone threshold (typically 10% of Contract Value) and finishes before 80%:

Deduction Rate = Advance Value ÷ [Contract Value × (80% - 10%)]

This shields contractors from early cash depletion during low-volume earthworks and mobilization stages.

ZATCA VAT Treatment: Advance Payments vs. Amortization Deductions

A frequent audit defect identified by project accountants in Saudi Arabia relates to the calculation of Value Added Tax (15%) when amortizing advance payments. Under ZATCA regulations:

  • Advance Payment Tax Invoice: The contractor must issue a tax invoice for the 15% VAT upon receiving the initial advance payment. Therefore, VAT has already been paid to ZATCA on the advance sum upfront.
  • Interim Payment Certificate (IPC) Invoicing: On subsequent monthly billings, the Advance Amortization deduction is treated as an adjustment to the gross work done. The contractor calculates VAT on the net billable milestone (Gross Work minus Advance Recovery) to prevent double taxation of the recovered advance amount.
  • Retention Holdback Note: Retention deductions (typically 5% or 10%) are security guarantees and do not reduce the immediate VAT tax base unless stipulated under certified cash accounting rules. VAT is applied to the gross work net of advance amortization.

Step-by-Step Anatomy of a Saudi IPC Calculation

Step 1
Gross Work Verified

Quantity surveyor approves physical completion of items (BoQ items + Variations).

Step 2
Advance Deducted

Standard 10% (or contract rate) deducted from gross work to repay client advance.

Step 3
Retention Withheld

Typically 10% withheld as defect liability security until Taking-Over Certificate (TOC).

Step 4
VAT Added

15% ZATCA VAT applied to net certificate value to determine final bank wire transfer.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is an Advance Payment Guarantee (APG) and when is it required?

An Advance Payment Guarantee (APG) is an irrevocable, unconditional letter of guarantee issued by a licensed Saudi bank on behalf of the contractor. Before the project owner releases any advance cash, the contractor must supply the APG equal to 100% of the advance amount. This ensures the client can reclaim unspent funds if the contractor defaults during early stages.

Can the contractor request a progressive reduction of the Bank Guarantee value?

Yes. Under Article 14.2 of FIDIC and standard Saudi commercial practice, as the contractor amortizes the advance through monthly IPC deductions, the bank guarantee's liability decreases. Most Saudi corporate banks (SNB, Al Rajhi, Riyad Bank, SAB) allow guarantee amount reductions upon receiving a formal verification certificate signed by the supervising project consultant and client confirming the unamortized balance.

What happens if the project is terminated prior to full advance recovery?

If a contract is terminated under Saudi GTPL or FIDIC Clause 15/16, the outstanding balance of the unrecovered advance becomes immediately due and payable to the employer. If the contractor cannot pay within the required contractual window (usually 14 to 28 days), the employer has the unilateral right to encash the remaining balance on the Advance Payment Guarantee.
