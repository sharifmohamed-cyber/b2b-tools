In-Depth Guide to Sick Leave Regulations Under Saudi Labor Law (Article 117)

Handling employee sickness and payroll continuity is one of the most critical responsibilities for human resources and payroll operations teams across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA). Governed by Royal Decree No. M/51, the Saudi Labor Law outlines strict, non-negotiable statutory protections for workers who fall ill or suffer non-work-related physical incapacity. Central to these protections is Article 117 (المادة السابعة عشرة بعد المائة), which establishes a tiered pay scale covering up to 120 days of sick leave within any single contractual service year.

Failing to compute Article 117 sick leave correctly can expose an enterprise to severe labor violation penalties under the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) wage protection enforcement, as well as contractual labor tribunal disputes. This guide details the four statutory tiers, cumulative calculation mechanics, medical authentication rules, and interaction with annual leave and contract termination.

Tier 1: Days 1 – 30
100% Full Pay

The employee is legally entitled to their complete wage with zero deductions for the first 30 days of authenticated medical absence throughout their contractual employment year, whether taken continuously or intermittently.

Tier 2: Days 31 – 90
75% Pay (Three-Quarters)

For the subsequent 60 calendar days, the employee receives three-quarters (75%) of their wage. The payroll department deducts exactly 25% of the daily wage rate for each day taken within this 60-day bracket.

Tier 3: Days 91 – 120
0% (Unpaid Leave)

For the following 30 calendar days, the employee retains job protection and healthcare insurance coverage, but the employer has no statutory obligation to pay cash wages. The daily rate is deducted at 100%.

The Mathematical Payroll Formulation

Payroll engines and accounting professionals compute Article 117 deductions utilizing two core variables: the Determined Daily Wage Rate and the Bracket Allocation Split.

# 1. Daily Wage Computation:
Daily Rate = Monthly Wage Basis / Daily Divisor (Normally 30 days)
// Gross Wage Basis = Basic Salary + Housing Allowance + Transport Allowance
# 2. Sick Leave Deduction Calculation:
Deduction = (Tier 1 Days × Daily Rate × 0.00)
            + (Tier 2 Days × Daily Rate × 0.25)
            + (Tier 3 Days × Daily Rate × 1.00)
            + (Tier 4 Exceeded Days × Daily Rate × 1.00)
# 3. Net Payable Salary for Cycle:
Net Period Pay = Monthly Gross Wage - Deduction - GOSI Contributions - Other Deductions

Critical Operational & Legal Nuances for HR Teams

1. Single Service Year vs. Calendar Year

A widespread payroll error in Saudi Arabia is resetting sick leave balances on January 1st of every calendar year. Under Article 117, the 120-day quota operates strictly on the employee's contractual service year (beginning from the date of joining or contractual renewal date), unless enterprise internal regulations approved by MHRSD explicitly state otherwise in favor of the employee.

2. Continuous vs. Intermittent Leave (التتابع والتجزئة)

Article 117 states that the 120 days apply "whether these leaves are continuous or intermittent" (سواءً أكانت هذه الإجازات متصلة أم متقطعة). An employee who takes 3 days in March, 10 days in June, and 20 days in September must have those days aggregated chronologically. The 31st cumulative day triggers the 75% rate, regardless of the gap between episodes.

3. Medical Authentication via Seha Platform (منصة صحة)

Medical excuses issued on arbitrary paper clinic slips are no longer legally compliant for labor dispute defense. The Kingdom’s Ministry of Health and MHRSD mandate electronic reports generated via the Seha digital portal, which automatically synchronizes with the Qiwa and GOSI platforms. Employers must authenticate the SL-Code (Sick Leave Code) before approving payroll disbursements.

4. Contract Termination Restrictions (Article 82)

Under Article 82 of the Saudi Labor Law, an employer is strictly prohibited from terminating an employee’s contract on the grounds of health or illness prior to the employee fully exhausting the 120-day statutory sick leave quota. Furthermore, the employee retains the right to request attaching their accumulated paid annual vacation days to their sick leave before any termination proceeding may be initiated.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does sick leave deduction include housing and transportation allowances? ↓

According to Article 2 of the Saudi Labor Law, the term "Wage" (الأجر) encompasses the actual basic salary plus all regular increments and allowances allocated to the worker. Unless internal organizational bylaws specify otherwise with Ministry certification, deductions for 75% and unpaid days are computed on the total regular wage.

Can an employer combine sick leave with annual leave? ↓

Yes. Article 82 grants the employee the legal prerogative to connect their accrued annual leave balance to their sick leave. If an employee exhausts the 30 full-pay days and 60 partial-pay days, they can consume paid annual leave rather than entering the unpaid 30-day tier.

What happens if the sick leave is caused by a workplace injury (Occupational Hazard)? ↓

Work injuries and occupational diseases do not follow Article 117. Instead, they fall under Article 137 of the Labor Law and GOSI Occupational Hazard regulations. For work-related injuries, the employee is entitled to 100% full wage for 60 days, followed by 75% wage paid directly by GOSI during medical rehabilitation up to one full year or permanent disability declaration.

Can an employee be dismissed while on approved sick leave? ↓

No. Article 82 specifically forbids termination during the sick leave entitlement period of 120 days. Any dismissal executed during this statutory timeframe is deemed arbitrary dismissal under Article 77, obligating the employer to pay statutory indemnities.
