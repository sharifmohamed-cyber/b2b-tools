Comprehensive Guide to Saudi MISA Licensing & Foreign Investment Costs

A complete operational breakdown for multinational corporations, regional ventures, and foreign entrepreneurs expanding into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia under Vision 2030.

01. MISA Statutory Fees

For standard Services, Industrial, and Contracting licenses, MISA charges an initial SAR 2,000 for the first year licensing application, followed by an annual subscription fee of SAR 60,000 per year. Entrepreneurial tech ventures enjoy reduced rates during their incubation phases.

02. Commercial Registration (CR)

Ministry of Commerce (MoC) standard LLC registration is SAR 1,200 for the primary establishment, with secondary city branches costing SAR 600 each. Chamber of Commerce membership is tiered based on legal capital and corporate activity, running between SAR 800 and SAR 10,000 annually.

03. Investor & GM Visas

Foreign shareholders and general managers receive accelerated investor visa allocation through the MISA portal. The MoFA entry visa fee is SAR 2,000 per key executive, excluding Muqeem residency Iqama issuance and corporate medical insurance policies.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can a foreign national own 100% of a company in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Under current Saudi investment regulations, foreign investors can own 100% of companies across most services, consulting, industrial, and technology sectors without a local Saudi partner.

What are the capital requirements for a services company?

The New Saudi Companies Law eliminated strict minimum capital figures for standard Limited Liability Companies (LLC). However, opening corporate bank accounts and obtaining initial investor visas generally requires at least SAR 10,000 to SAR 500,000.

What is the Regional Headquarters (RHQ) licensing regime?

Multinationals managing MENA regional operations from Riyadh can secure an RHQ license, granting zero-rate corporate income tax for 30 years and exemptions from Saudization quotas for specific executive positions.

Are MISA fees subject to 15% VAT?

Statutory sovereign government ministry licensing fees are typically outside the scope of VAT or exempt. However, professional attestation, consulting retainers, and translation services carry standard 15% ZATCA VAT.
