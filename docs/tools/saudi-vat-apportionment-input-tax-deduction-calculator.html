Comprehensive Guide to Saudi VAT Partial Exemption & Apportionment

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, the Value Added Tax (VAT) system operates under the legal framework established by Royal Decree No. (M/113) and the Implementing Regulations issued by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA). Under general principles, a taxable enterprise is entitled to deduct input tax paid on its purchases and expenses, provided those costs are incurred in making taxable supplies (both standard-rated at 15% and zero-rated at 0%).

However, businesses operating in sectors such as financial services, real estate (residential leasing), healthcare, education, or insurance often generate a mix of taxable supplies and exempt supplies. Under Article 51 of the Saudi VAT Implementing Regulations, input VAT directly associated with exempt activities is strictly non-deductible. When input VAT is incurred on general overheads that serve both activities—such as ERP subscriptions, IT infrastructure, audit fees, or headquarters lease—the enterprise must perform Proportional Input Tax Deduction Apportionment.

Mathematical Formula for Proportional Input Tax Recovery (Article 51)

ZATCA mandates the standard turnover-based apportionment calculation to determine the recovery fraction of residual/overhead input VAT:

Apportionment Ratio (P%) = [ Taxable Supplies (Standard + Zero-Rated) / Total Eligible Supplies (Taxable + Exempt) ] × 100
  • Numerator: The value of supplies made during the period that give rise to input VAT deduction (15% standard supplies + 0% zero-rated supplies + qualifying out-of-scope supplies with deduction rights).
  • Denominator: Total value of all economic supplies made in the period (Taxable supplies + Exempt supplies).
  • Exclusions: Article 51 explicitly excludes capital assets sales (e.g., selling office property or company machinery), supplies from non-economic activities, and internal branch transfers from both numerator and denominator to avoid distorting the ongoing operational recovery ratio.

Three-Bucket Input VAT Classification Methodology

Saudi registered entities must maintain audited evidence segmenting all incurred input VAT into three distinct streams before filing Form VAT201:

Bucket 1: Direct Taxable (100%)

Input VAT on goods or services used exclusively to execute standard-rated or zero-rated activities. 100% deductible on line 7 or 8 of the VAT return.

Bucket 2: Direct Exempt (0%)

Input VAT incurred strictly to make exempt supplies (e.g., refurbishment of residential rental apartments). 0% deductible; expensed in the P&L.

Bucket 3: Residual / Common (P%)

Overheads, shared operational costs, and general corporate expenses. Multiplied by the Apportionment Ratio (P%) to derive allowable deduction.

Year-End Annual Wash-Up (Reconciliation Adjustment)

Throughout the financial year, monthly or quarterly taxpayers apply either the previous tax year's proportional ratio or the current periodic ratio as a provisional deduction. At the end of the 4th tax period (Quarter 4 or December return), Article 51 requires a mandatory full-year recalculation.

The final actual annual recovery ratio is calculated across the entire 12-month turnover. If the provisional deductions claimed exceed the true annual entitlement, the taxpayer must repay the difference to ZATCA. Conversely, if provisional claims were lower than actual annual entitlement, the entity is eligible for a credit adjustment.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can an entity use an alternative apportionment method instead of turnover? ▼

Yes. Under Article 51(7) of the VAT Implementing Regulations, a taxable person may apply in writing to ZATCA to approve an alternative method (e.g., headcount basis, floor area apportionment, input cost ratio, or transaction count) if the standard turnover method does not provide a fair and reasonable reflection of overhead resource usage. Written ZATCA approval is legally mandatory before applying any alternative method.

Are bank financing interest and residential rental income exempt from Saudi VAT? ▼

Yes. Under Article 29 and Article 30, financial services where consideration is an implicit margin, spread, or interest (including Murabaha profit margins and loan interest), as well as residential real estate leases, are exempt supplies. They must be included in the denominator of the apportionment formula.

How does ZATCA treat capital asset adjustments (Article 52)? ▼

Capital assets with a purchase cost equal to or exceeding SAR 50,000 are subject to multi-year adjustment rules: 6 years for tangible moveable capital assets (equipment, IT servers, vehicles) and 10 years for immovable property (buildings). If the taxable use ratio changes over this tracking lifecycle, annual adjustments must be booked.
