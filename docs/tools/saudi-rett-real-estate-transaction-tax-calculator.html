Executive Guide: Saudi Real Estate Transaction Tax (RETT / ضريبة التصرفات العقارية)

In October 2020, through Royal Decree No. (A/84), the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia radically overhauled real estate fiscal policy by carving out all immovable property transactions from the standard 15% Value Added Tax (VAT) framework and establishing a separate, dedicated Real Estate Transaction Tax (RETT) levied at a flat statutory rate of 5%. This reform was primarily enacted to stimulate residential homeownership for Saudi citizens (supporting Vision 2030 targets), lower market frictional costs, and provide commercial liquidity to commercial development and corporate restructuring transactions.

Administered strictly by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) in collaboration with the Ministry of Justice (Najiz), RETT is a transactional tax that attaches to the gross value of real estate disposition—whether land, residential housing, commercial office towers, industrial facilities, or long-term leaseholds exceeding 50 years. Understanding the operational mechanism, exact exemption categories, and broker VAT interactions is essential for Chief Financial Officers, corporate legal counsels, and individual home buyers.

Core Mathematical Calculations & Statutory Logic

The calculation methodology adheres strictly to Executive Regulations issued under Resolution No. (3805) and subsequent Ministry of Finance updates:

Standard Property Transaction:
Gross RETT = Value × 0.05
Where Value = MAX (Agreed Contract Price, Fair Market Valuation).
Citizen First-Home Purchase:
Relief = MIN (Value, 1,000,000) × 0.05
Net RETT = MAX (0, (Value - 1,000,000) × 0.05). Maximum state burden is SAR 50,000.
Brokerage & Service VAT:
Broker Commission = Value × Rate (max 2.5%)
Broker VAT = Commission × 0.15 (Standard VAT applies to brokerage, NOT RETT).
Total Buyer Cash Settlement:
Total = Value + Net RETT + Commission + VAT
All disbursements payable simultaneously at notarization or Najiz release.

Statutory Exemptions under ZATCA Article 3

Not all real estate title transfers incur the 5% tax. ZATCA maintains an exhaustive list of legally qualified tax exemptions. Filing a formal waiver request on ZATCA's e-portal is mandatory prior to notarization:

Exemption Clause Legal Description Mandatory Documentation
Family Gift (هبة عائلية) Property donated gratuitously without monetary consideration to a spouse or relative up to the 3rd degree (parents, children, grandparents, siblings, grandchildren). Absher / Family record proving familial kinship within allowable degrees.
Inheritance Distribution (قسمة التركات) Division and formal distribution of bequeathed estate real estate among legal heirs in accordance with Sharia inheritance deeds. Inheritance Deed (حصر ورثة) & Estate Partition ruling from Ministry of Justice.
In-Kind Capital Contribution (حصة عينية) Transferring real property to a Saudi capital company in exchange for corporate shares, on condition shares are not sold for 5 continuous years. Ministry of Commerce Articles of Association & independent certified valuer (Taqeem) report.
Mergers & Demergers (اندماج وتقسيم) Corporate structural reorganization where titles move between legal entities under common control or statutory mergers. Certified commercial register and statutory corporate restructuring resolutions.
Charitable Waqf (وقف خيري أو أهلي) Transfer of deed to an accredited philanthropic organization or endowed family trust recognized by the General Authority for Awqaf. Valid Endowment Deed registered with the General Authority for Awqaf.

The Accredited Real Estate Developer VAT Refund Mechanism

Because real estate sales are exempt from 15% VAT (attracting 5% RETT instead), developers would ordinarily suffer "tax cascading"—meaning they cannot recover the 15% VAT paid on subcontractors, civil works, architectural consulting, and building materials (cement, rebar, finishes). To eliminate this burden, ZATCA established the Eligible Real Estate Developers Refund Scheme (برنامج المطورين العقاريين المرخصين).

Under this special facility, licensed developers registered with the Wafi program and ZATCA can submit periodic refund claims to recoup 100% of the input VAT incurred on residential property developments. This preserves developer margins, prevents cost inflation on off-plan and ready-built housing, and ensures Saudi real estate supply remains competitive.

Frequently Asked Questions (ZATCA & Najiz Guidance)

Who is legally responsible for paying the 5% RETT to ZATCA?

Under ZATCA regulations, the Seller (Disposer / المتصرف) is legally liable for registering the transaction on the ZATCA portal and settling the RETT SADAD invoice. However, in standard market practice, contractual agreements typically stipulate that the Buyer pays the tax directly into the generated ZATCA SADAD bill on behalf of the seller prior to notarization at the Ministry of Justice. Both parties remain jointly accountable until the SADAD bill is settled.

How does the first-time Saudi homebuyer exemption work if the home costs SAR 1,400,000?

By Royal Decree, the state pays the 5% tax on the first SAR 1,000,000 of the residential purchase price. For a home valued at SAR 1,400,000, the total 5% RETT would normally be SAR 70,000. The government covers SAR 50,000 (5% of SAR 1,000,000), and the citizen homebuyer only pays SAR 20,000 (5% on the remaining SAR 400,000).

Does RETT apply to off-plan sales (بيع على الخارطة) under the Wafi program?

Yes, off-plan sales under Wafi are subject to the 5% RETT. However, payment terms are aligned with construction escrow disbursements, and qualified off-plan buyers who are first-time citizen home buyers can still apply their SAR 1,000,000 exemption certificate to the contract value.

What are the penalties for under-declaring real estate value to avoid RETT?

Deliberately declaring a property sale value lower than the actual consideration exchanged or fair market value is classified as statutory tax evasion. ZATCA penalizes offenders with fines ranging between 100% and 300% of the unpaid tax difference, alongside deed invalidation proceedings and potential criminal prosecution.
