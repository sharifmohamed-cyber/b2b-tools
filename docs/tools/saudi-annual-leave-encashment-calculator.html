Saudi Labor Law: Article 109 & 111 Mandates

Under Article 109 of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia Labor Law (Royal Decree No. M/51), every worker is entitled to a prepaid annual leave of no less than 21 calendar days per annum. This mandatory entitlement rises to a minimum of 30 calendar days once the employee completes five consecutive years of service with the same employer.

Furthermore, Article 111 governs leave encashment upon end of service: "A worker shall be entitled to cash pay for accrued leave days that have not been availed of for the period of his work, computed on the basis of the last wage received by the worker." This applies whether the contract ends due to resignation, non-renewal, redundancy, or mutual termination.

The "Actual Wage" vs. "Basic Wage" Distinction

A frequent source of labor disputes before Saudi Labor Courts (Mahakim Al-Omaliah) is the daily wage divisor basis. According to Article 2 of the Saudi Labor Law, "Wage" refers to the Actual Wage (الأجر الفعلي), which encompasses the basic salary in addition to all recurrent, contractual allowances such as housing (بدل سكن), transport (بدل مواصلات), and guaranteed bonuses.

Unless explicitly restricted to basic salary by unambiguous contractual clauses sanctioned by MHRSD bylaws, leave encashment must be settled using the full gross monthly salary divided by thirty (30) days.

Official Calculation Formulas & Methodology

1. Actual Daily Wage Daily Rate = (Basic + Housing + Transport + Fixed Allowances) / 30
2. Pro-Rata Accrual Accrued Days = (Annual Entitlement / 365) × Days Worked in Cycle
3. Encashment Due Settlement (SAR) = Daily Wage × (Accrued Days - Taken Days)

Note on the 30-day divisor: While Gregorian calendar months vary between 28 and 31 days, standard Saudi payroll convention and court precedent define the standard daily divisor as 30 days unless the employment contract specifies exact days in the month (calendar divisor). Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can an employer force an employee to forfeit unused leave in Saudi Arabia?

No. Under Article 109 and Article 111, leave is a statutory right. While an employer may schedule the timing of the annual leave according to work requirements or postpone it to the following year with written employee consent (Article 110), the worker cannot be forced to forfeit compensation for unavailed leave upon separation.

Does leave encashment attract GOSI deductions?

No. General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) social insurance contributions apply to monthly contributory wages (Basic salary + Housing allowance). Terminal leave encashment paid upon exit is categorized as an accrued settlement indemnity, not an active contributory wage, and is therefore exempt from GOSI deductions.

Can an employee encash leave days while still working (without leaving)?

Yes, if both employer and employee mutually agree in writing and the internal company policies approve mid-service encashment. However, the Saudi Labor Law emphasizes the physical rest requirement, specifying that employees must take at least 15 consecutive days in the due year and cannot postpone the entirety of their leave indefinitely.

What happens if the employee resigns before completing their first year?

Under Article 111, the employee is entitled to pro-rata compensation for the fraction of the year worked. For example, if an employee worked 6 months under a 21-day entitlement contract and took 0 leave, they are entitled to 10.5 days of leave pay at their final wage rate upon exit.
