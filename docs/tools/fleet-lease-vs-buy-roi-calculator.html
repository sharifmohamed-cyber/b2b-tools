Strategic Guide: Fleet Capital Expenditure (CapEx) vs. Operating Lease (OpEx)

Understanding the mechanics of Net Present Value, IFRS 16 impact, and cash preservation for enterprise fleets.

1. The Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Model

Corporate fleet acquisition decisions cannot rely merely on sticker price versus monthly rental quotes. The true economic cost encompasses acquisition financing, scheduled maintenance cycles, tire replacement overhead, comprehensive insurance, administrative tracking, and terminal salvage price risk.

When purchasing outright or via bank debt, an enterprise takes full risk on the secondary market value of the vehicle after 3 to 5 years. In an operating lease, this residual risk remains completely with the fleet management leasing lessor.

2. Discounted Cash Flows (NPV) & WACC

Money spent today is more expensive than money committed four years in the future. By applying your company's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), our engine calculates the Net Present Value (NPV) of all outflows under both options:

NPV = Σ [ Net Outflow_t / (1 + WACC)^t ] - Day0_Equity

Preserving day-one liquidity through leasing enables companies to invest working capital into core business activities yielding higher margins than depreciating fleet assets.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does IFRS 16 affect lease vs buy?

Under IFRS 16, virtually all leases must be recognized on balance sheet as a Right-of-Use (ROU) asset. However, operational and cash flow advantages remain distinct.

What is the Corporate Tax Shield formula applied?

Tax shields reduce net cash outflow: Tax Shield = Allowable Expense * Corporate Income Tax Rate.
