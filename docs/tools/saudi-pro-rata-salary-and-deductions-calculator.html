The Ultimate Guide to Pro-Rata Salary Calculations Under Saudi Labor Law

In Saudi Arabia, administering partial month payroll is one of the most contentious operational challenges faced by HR generalists, payroll accountants, and enterprise ERP implementers. Whether an employee joins mid-month, resigns prior to the payroll cut-off, takes unauthorized leaves of absence, or agrees to unpaid leave under Article 116 of the Saudi Labor Law, calculating exact pro-rata remuneration demands rigorous compliance with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) guidelines, the Saudi Labor Law (Royal Decree No. M/51), and the General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI).

1. The 30-Day Rule vs. Calendar Days: Legal Foundations (Article 131)

Under Article 131 of the Saudi Labor Law, whenever wages or contractual entitlements are designated by the month or year without specifying exact calendar variations, a calendar month is legally deemed to comprise 30 days. Consequently, in the absence of an explicit employment clause stating otherwise, the statutory daily wage formula is:

Statutory Daily Wage Rate = Total Monthly Wage (Basic + Guaranteed Allowances) ÷ 30 Days

However, enterprises often operate using one of three widespread accounting methodologies:

  • Standard 30-Day Divisor (Recommended by Labor Courts): Daily wage is calculated by dividing monthly compensation by 30, regardless of whether the month has 28, 29, 30, or 31 days. Worked days are credited based on remaining calendar days in that month.
  • Actual Calendar Days Divisor: Divides the monthly wage by the exact number of calendar days in that specific month (e.g., 31 in January, 28 in February). While mathematically intuitive, it yields varying daily rates throughout the year for identical base salaries.
  • Actual Working Days Divisor (Net Working Days): Excludes scheduled weekly rest days (Fridays/Saturdays). This method is occasionally applied in non-standard consultancy or hourly contracts, but must be explicitly documented in the company's approved Internal Work Regulations (لائحة تنظيم العمل الداخلية).

2. Joiners, Leavers, and Unpaid Leave (Article 116)

Mid-Month Joiners: If an employee’s contract commences on April 16 in a 30-day month, they are entitled to pay for 15 days (April 16 through 30 inclusive), including the weekly rest days falling within that period.

Unpaid Leave (Article 116): With the employer’s explicit written consent, an employee may take leave without pay. In this circumstance, the employment contract is considered legally suspended beyond 20 days unless otherwise agreed. For payroll deduction purposes, each day of unpaid leave reduces gross remuneration by one standard daily wage (Monthly Package ÷ 30).

Weekly Rest Day Entitlement (Article 89): Employees are entitled to at least one full day of paid weekly rest (commonly two days in modern 40-hour/48-hour schemes). Mid-month calculations must not deduct scheduled weekly rest days unless the employee was absent without authorization both immediately preceding and following that rest period, pursuant to standard disciplinary regulations.

3. GOSI Contribution Mechanics on Partial Month Wages

A frequent source of payroll discrepancy involves social insurance deductions. Under GOSI regulations:

  • Contributory Wage Definition: GOSI calculates contributions based on Basic Salary + Housing Allowance (fixed monthly allowance or 2 months basic equivalent if company-provided), capped at a statutory ceiling of 45,000 SAR per month.
  • Full Month Rule in GOSI Portal: GOSI's portal often records full monthly contribution liabilities when an employee is registered as active for any portion of the contribution month, unless reported through specific entry/exit date APIs. When prorating employee take-home pay, companies typically deduct the full employee share or a pro-rata share based on the company's GOSI reconciliation practice.
  • Contribution Percentages: For Saudi nationals, the employee contribution is 9.75% (9% Annuities + 0.75% SANED Unemployment Insurance). The employer contributes 11.75% (9% Annuities + 0.75% SANED + 2% Occupational Hazards). For expatriates, the employee pays 0%, while the employer pays 2% for Occupational Hazards.

Practical Calculation Scenario: Mid-Month Joiner

Employee: Saudi National joining on the 11th of May (31-day month).

Compensation Package: Basic: 9,000 SAR | Housing: 3,000 SAR | Transport: 1,000 SAR | Total Gross: 13,000 SAR.

30-Day Basis Calculation:

  • Daily Rate = 13,000 ÷ 30 = 433.33 SAR / day
  • Payable Days (May 11 to 31) = 21 calendar days
  • Earned Gross Salary = 21 × 433.33 = 9,099.93 SAR
  • GOSI Contributory Base (Basic + Housing) = 12,000 SAR
  • Pro-Rata GOSI Base = (12,000 ÷ 30) × 21 = 8,400.00 SAR
  • Employee GOSI Deduction (9.75%) = 8,400 × 0.0975 = 819.00 SAR
  • Net Payable Take-Home Salary: 9,099.93 - 819.00 = 8,280.93 SAR

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does Saudi Labor Law mandate 30 days for salary deductions in February and 31-day months?

Yes. Article 131 states that unless expressly agreed otherwise, the month is legally treated as 30 days. Most labor courts and HR auditors enforce 30 days as the default denominator to avoid penalizing employees during shorter months (February) or creating arbitrary rate swings in 31-day months.

Can an employer deduct weekends if an employee takes unpaid leave?

If an employee takes approved unpaid leave from Sunday to Thursday (5 working days), the subsequent Friday and Saturday (statutory rest days) should not be deducted unless the leave explicitly spans over contiguous weeks as specified in the agreed unpaid leave request form.

How does pro-rata payroll integrate with the Saudi Wage Protection System (WPS)?

Under the MHRSD Wage Protection System (نظام حماية الأجور - مداد / منصة قوى), variances between the registered contract salary and the net bank transfer that exceed 20% will trigger a compliance justification flag. When uploading partial-month SIF files, HR must log appropriate deduction reason codes (e.g., unpaid leave or mid-month start) to avoid green/platinum Nitaqat rating flags.
