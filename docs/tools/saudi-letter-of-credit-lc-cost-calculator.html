Comprehensive Corporate Guide to Saudi Letters of Credit (LC) & Trade Finance Costs

Detailed breakdown of SAMA corporate banking tariffs, UCP 600 rules, Usance vs. Sight mechanisms, and ZATCA tax rules for procurement in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

1. Fundamentals of Trade Finance & Letters of Credit in Saudi Arabia

A Letter of Credit (LC), governed internationally by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) and domestically by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), is an irrevocable undertaking issued by an issuing bank at the request of an applicant (importer/buyer) in Saudi Arabia. The bank pledges to pay the beneficiary (exporter/seller) provided conforming documents are submitted within stipulated deadlines. In Saudi Arabia's massive Vision 2030 industrial, infrastructure, and wholesale trading expansion, LCs remain the premier financial instrument for mitigating cross-border credit and performance risks.

2. Key Cost Components and Mathematical Formulas

Saudi commercial banks (such as Al Rajhi, SNB, Riyad Bank, Alinma, SAB, and Banque Saudi Fransi) structure their documentary credit fees through several distinct commission buckets:

A. LC Issuance Commission

Charged per quarter (or fraction of 90 days). If an LC is valid for 180 days, two quarters are assessed:

Issuance Fee = LC Value × (Rate / 100) × Quarters

Subject to bank minimums (commonly SAR 450 - SAR 750).

B. Acceptance Commission (Usance LCs)

Charged when the bank accepts a deferred draft (e.g., 90 or 180 days post-Bill of Lading):

Acceptance = LC Value × (Annual % / 360) × Tenor Days

Reflects bank credit risk during the deferred payment tenor.

C. Advising, Confirmation & SWIFT

Fixed handling charges plus confirmation coverage if the overseas seller requests an overseas confirming bank:

Operational = SWIFT + Courier + Document Exam

D. ZATCA Value Added Tax (VAT 15%)

Under Saudi VAT regulations, bank service commissions are standard-rated at 15% (margin interest on funded Murabaha is exempt):

VAT (15%) = Sum of (Issuance + Acceptance + Operational) × 0.15

3. Cash Margin Collateral vs. Bank Credit Facilities

Saudi banks determine the cash margin (0% to 100%) based on the corporate client's credit rating, annual audited financial statements, and existing credit line limits. High-credit Tier-1 contractors and listed Saudi Joint Stock companies frequently secure 0% to 10% cash margins, preserving liquidity. In contrast, startups, SMEs, and newly incorporated commercial entities under MISA may be asked for 50% to 100% cash cover blocked in a non-interest-bearing or term collateral account until the LC documents are negotiated. Factoring this collateral requirement into total working capital cycles is vital for cash flow solvency.

Frequently Asked Questions (Trade Finance in KSA)

What is the difference between a Sight LC and a Usance LC in Saudi Arabia? ▼

In a Sight LC, the issuing bank releases payment to the supplier immediately upon receipt and verification of compliant shipping documents (usually within 5 banking days under UCP 600). In a Usance LC (deferred payment), payment is deferred for an agreed credit tenor (e.g., 60, 90, or 180 days after the Bill of Lading date), allowing the Saudi buyer time to clear customs, sell inventory, and collect receivables before paying the bank.

Are bank trade finance commissions subject to 15% ZATCA VAT? ▼

Yes. Under ZATCA regulations, all explicit banking service fees, including LC issuance, amendments, acceptance, document examination, and SWIFT fees, are subject to the standard 15% VAT rate. However, pure interest margins (or Murabaha profit markups under Sharia finance) are generally VAT-exempt under financial services rules.

What is a UPAS LC (Usance Payable at Sight)? ▼

A UPAS LC provides the best of both worlds: the foreign seller gets paid at sight by a financing bank, while the Saudi buyer enjoys a deferred usance repayment period (e.g., 90 or 180 days). The interest/discount charge is borne by either party depending on the commercial agreement.

How does a Letter of Credit differ from a Bank Guarantee (LG)? ▼

An LC is a primary payment mechanism used to finance trade contracts upon performance. A Bank Guarantee (Letter of Guarantee / LG) is a secondary or contingent instrument (Bid Bond, Performance Bond, Advance Payment Bond) designed to pay only in case of default or breach of contract.
