Complete Operational Guide to Saudi Import VAT, Customs Duties & Fasah Reconciliation

A technical handbook for Saudi CFOs, tax compliance officers, supply chain directors, and chartered accountants navigating ZATCA import declaration Box 7/Box 8 rules and landed cost valuation.

1 Understanding the Valuation Formula for Saudi Import Duties and VAT

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, customs duties and import Value Added Tax (VAT) are calculated hierarchically under the unified framework of the GCC Common Customs Law and Saudi ZATCA (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority) statutes. A frequent error encountered by procurement teams is calculating the standard 15% VAT exclusively on the commercial supplier invoice value. Under Saudi law, import VAT is charged on the total landed valuation at customs entry, which incorporates the Cost, Insurance, and Freight (CIF) value, plus statutory customs duty, plus any applicable excise or port surcharges.

Step 1 (CIF Customs Valuation): CIF Value = FOB Commercial Invoice + Cross-border Freight + Marine Insurance
Step 2 (Customs Duty Assessment): Customs Duty (SAR) = CIF Value × HS Tariff Rate (%)
Step 3 (ZATCA Import VAT Base): VAT Assessment Base = CIF Value + Customs Duty + Any Excise/Tariff Surcharges
Step 4 (Total Import VAT Payable): Import VAT (SAR) = VAT Assessment Base × 15%
Step 5 (Total Landed Inventory Cost): Inventory Landed Cost = CIF + Duty + Fasah Port Fees + Broker Fees + Non-recoverable VAT

2 ZATCA VAT Return Architecture: Box 7 vs. Box 8 Reconciliation

Taxpayers registered for VAT in Saudi Arabia file quarterly or monthly returns via the ERCA/ZATCA portal. When clearing imported goods through Saudi seaports (e.g., King Abdulaziz Port in Dammam, Jeddah Islamic Port) or land borders (e.g., King Fahd Causeway, Batha border), companies generally fall under one of two operational filing streams:

  • Box 7 (Imports subject to VAT paid at Customs): This is the default mechanism for most businesses. The importer or their customs broker settles the 15% VAT and customs duty prior to container release via SADAD. On the subsequent ZATCA VAT Return, the net tax is pre-populated in Box 7, allowing the registrant to reclaim 100% of the tax paid as an input tax deduction in Box 14, provided the goods are utilized in taxable economic activity.
  • Box 8 (Imports subject to VAT accounted for through reverse charge / Postponement): Qualified large enterprises holding formal postponement authorization from ZATCA are permitted to defer VAT settlement at port gates to optimize working capital. Under this system, VAT is accounted for via the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) directly within Box 8, generating an offsetting input/output entry without cash outflow at port customs.

3 Treatment of Customs Brokerage Fees, Demurrage, and Port Logistics

A critical distinction must be maintained between border-level customs taxes (collected directly by ZATCA Customs) and ancillary clearing services provided by freight forwarders, terminal operators (Mawani), and Fasah digital platforms. Domestic clearing services are standard-rated domestic transactions. The customs broker will issue a local e-Invoice (Fatoora) containing their professional service fee plus 15% domestic VAT.

However, any disbursements paid by the customs broker on the importer's behalf (such as government duty receipts or port inspection charges) represent pure principal-agent balance-sheet disbursements. Under ZATCA e-Invoicing guidelines, customs agents cannot add duplicate VAT to governmental duty amounts already settled on the Bayan.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What documents are required to support an input VAT deduction for imports in Saudi Arabia?

Importers must archive three mandatory records for at least six years: (1) The official Customs Declaration document (Bayan) issued by ZATCA displaying the importer’s 15-digit VAT number, (2) The SADAD payment receipt or customs settlement voucher verifying payment, and (3) The commercial invoice and international Bill of Lading (B/L) confirming supplier details.

Why does ZATCA Box 7 occasionally differ from internal ERP accounting ledgers?

Discrepancies typically occur due to foreign exchange differences between the supplier invoice booking date and the official Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) exchange rate applied by customs on the date the Bayan is processed, or when clearing agents group multiple invoices under a single unified declaration without line-level itemization.

Is demurrage and port container storage subject to VAT in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Terminal storage, container detention, and handling fees invoiced by shipping lines or Saudi port authorities within the Kingdom are classified as taxable domestic services subject to the standard 15% VAT rate, which can be claimed as input tax subject to regular compliance rules.
