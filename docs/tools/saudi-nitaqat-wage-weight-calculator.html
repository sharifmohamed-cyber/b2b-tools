The Comprehensive Guide to Saudi Nitaqat Wage Thresholds & Saudization Weight Calculation

Under the regulations set forth by the Saudi Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), Saudization (Nitaqat / نطاقات) calculations do not rely merely on raw employment headcounts. Instead, each Saudi employee registered under the General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) is assigned a specific weighted coefficient ranging from 0.0 to 4.0 depending on their gross registered monthly wage, physical condition, and labor contract type.

1. Minimum Wage Tiers for Saudi Employee Point Weighting

The current ministerial decree dictates strict wage thresholds for counting a Saudi citizen in an establishment's nationalization ratio:

Gross Monthly Wage (GOSI) Nitaqat Point Weight Legal Classification Strategic HR Impact
SAR 4,000 or greater 1.0 Worker Full Saudization Weight Provides 100% quota credit in Qiwa.
SAR 3,000 to SAR 3,999.99 0.5 Worker Partial Weight Tier Requires two employees to equal one full quota credit.
Below SAR 3,000 0.0 Worker Non-Contributory No Nitaqat benefit; company incurs GOSI fees without quota gain.

2. Special Employee Classifications & Regulatory Quotas

To incentivize the employment of specific Saudi demographics, MHRSD provides enhanced multiplier points, provided the employee satisfies wage requirements (minimum SAR 4,000 for full benefit):

  • Persons with Special Needs (Disabilities): Counted as 4.0 workers in the Nitaqat system, provided their monthly salary is not less than SAR 4,000. However, this multiplier is subject to an enterprise cap of maximum 10% of total national headcount.
  • Released Inmates / Rehabilitated Prisoners: Counted as 2.0 workers for a continuous period of 24 months from the date of employment, also subject to the statutory 10% maximum cap.
  • Students (Part-Time): Counted as 0.5 workers with a monthly salary of at least SAR 3,000, subject to student employment caps relative to total Saudi workforce.
  • Flexible Work Contracts ('Mrn'): Aggregated based on total completed hourly timesheets. Every 168 certified hours accumulated within a month by Saudi flexible contract workers equal 1.0 full Saudization unit.

3. Mathematical Calculation Formula Used by Qiwa & MHRSD

Saudization Percentage (%) = [ Total Weighted Saudi Units / (Total Weighted Saudi Units + Total Expatriate Units) ] × 100

Where:
Total Weighted Saudi Units = (Σ Saudis ≥ 4000 × 1.0) + (Σ Saudis 3000-3999 × 0.5) + (Disabled Saudis × 4.0 [capped]) + (Flexible Hours / 168)

Note that Nitaqat evaluations on the official Qiwa platform do not rely solely on end-of-month point-in-time snapshots. They are based on a 26-week moving average (نطاقات الموزون) to prevent establishments from temporarily onboarding citizens right before submitting visa requests or renewal quotas. Any wage changes take effect across subsequent weekly snapshots.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if a Saudi employee earns SAR 3,800?

A Saudi employee registered at SAR 3,800 monthly wage contributes only 0.5 points to the Saudization percentage. By increasing their registered salary by just SAR 200 to meet the SAR 4,000 threshold, your establishment immediately doubles their point value to 1.0.

Does basic salary determine the Nitaqat weight or the total package?

The calculation is based on the total wage subject to GOSI deduction (الراتب الخاضع للاشتراك), which typically includes the Basic Salary + Housing Allowance (or equivalent monetary housing provision).

How do GCC nationals count in Nitaqat?

Citizens of Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries (Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Qatar, UAE) employed in Saudi Arabia are treated similarly to Saudi citizens regarding labor classification in most commercial sectors, subject to valid GOSI registration and minimum wage rules.

What is the penalty if an establishment drops into the Red or Low Green band?

Dropping to the Low Green or Red tier blocks immediate automated Qiwa services, including issuing new block work visas, transferring expat employee sponsorships into the company, and renewing Iqamas without special exemptions.
