Saudi Contractor Retention & Liquidated Damages: Complete Engineering & Accounting Guide

1. Retention Withholding Mechanics (محجوز الضمان)

In Saudi Arabia construction and engineering procurement, retention money acts as financial security to safeguard the employer (client) against defective workmanship, incomplete punch-list items, or contractor abandonment. Under standard Saudi contracting practices, employers withhold between 5% and 10% from each Interim Payment Certificate (IPC).

Release Schedule: The first 50% of retention is typically released upon the issuance of the Initial Handover Certificate (التسليم الابتدائي). The remaining 50% is retained throughout the Defects Liability Period (DLP - فترة الصيانة والضمان), usually 365 days, and released upon Final Handover (التسليم النهائي) and submission of Zakat & Tax clearance (ZATCA certificate).

2. Liquidated Damages & Delay Fines (غرامات التأخير)

Under the Saudi Government Tenders and Procurement Law (GTPL - نظام المنافسات والمشتريات الحكومية), liquidated damages are systematically tiered under Article 74. If a contractor exceeds the contractual completion date without an approved Extension of Time (EOT), delay fines accrue on an average daily cost formula.

Crucially, statutory regulations establish a maximum statutory ceiling (typically 10% for GTPL projects and 10% to 20% in private FIDIC contracts) of the total contract value. Once this threshold is reached, the employer may initiate default procedures, withdraw the project, or encash bank guarantees.

Standard Mathematical Formulas Used

Retention Amount: Retention = IPC Gross × Retention%
Advance Recovery: Recovery = Min(IPC Gross × Rate%, Balance)
GTPL Fine Tier: Daily Fine = (Contract Value ÷ Duration) × Tier%

3. ZATCA Value-Added Tax (VAT) Treatment on Retentions

A frequent point of audit dispute in Saudi Arabia is whether 15% VAT applies to the gross certified amount or the net payable after retention. Under ZATCA (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority) construction guidelines, VAT becomes due upon the issuance of the tax invoice or receipt of payment (whichever occurs earlier) based on the gross value of goods and services supplied. Retention money does not reduce the taxable base when the works are billed; rather, the full invoice value is subject to VAT at 15%. Liquidated damages deducted from invoices represent compensatory damage claims, which are outside the scope of VAT and do not reduce the original supply value.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can an employer deduct liquidated damages before project completion in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. If the contractor fails to achieve intermediate binding milestones or if delays exceed contractual grace periods, employers frequently withhold estimated liquidated damages from interim payments as a protective set-off, subject to final reconciliation upon project completion or EOT determination.

Can a contractor substitute cash retention with a bank guarantee in KSA?

Yes. Under Article 64 of the Saudi GTPL and standard FIDIC Red Book Sub-Clause 14.9, contractors can replace accumulated retention monies by furnishing an unconditional, irrevocable Bank Guarantee (ضمان بنكي للدفعة المحتجزة) issued by a licensed Saudi bank.

What is the maximum legal penalty cap under the Saudi Government Procurement Law?

Under Article 74 of the GTPL, the aggregate delay fines for public works and supply contracts cannot exceed 10% of the total contract value. For services and consulting contracts, the statutory ceiling is generally capped at 20%.
