Guide to ZATCA Dual-Tax Assessment for Mixed-Ownership Entities in Saudi Arabia

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, corporations with mixed equity shareholding—where shares are held jointly by Saudi or GCC nationals alongside non-GCC foreign investors—are subject to a dual statutory tax framework managed by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA). Rather than applying a single blended tax rate across the entity, ZATCA requires an analytical partition of the enterprise's tax and zakat obligations based on strictly defined shareholding percentages, calendar definitions, and balance sheet deductible assets. Understanding this dual mechanism is critical for CFOs, finance directors, audit managers, and tax consultants operating across Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, and foreign commercial joint ventures.

Under the Saudi Income Tax Law issued under Royal Decree No. M/1 and the Zakat Regulations enacted by Ministerial Resolution No. 2216, the enterprise must maintain audited accounts that allocate the tax base for the foreign shareholder and the zakat base for the Saudi/GCC shareholder.

Mathematical Formulas & Statutory Allocation Mechanics

1. Adjusted Taxable Net Profit Formulation:

Adjusted Net Profit = Net Book Profit + Disallowed Expenses + Depreciation Adjustments - Prior Tax Losses Utilized

2. Foreign Corporate Income Tax Calculation:

Foreign Tax Base = Adjusted Net Profit × Foreign Ownership Percentage (%) Foreign Tax Payable = Foreign Tax Base × 20.0%

3. Saudi Zakat Base Formulation (Net Financing Method):

Full Firm Zakat Base = (Paid Capital + Reserves + Retained Earnings + Term Loans) - (Net Fixed Assets + Long-term Investments) Saudi Apportioned Base = Full Firm Zakat Base × Saudi Ownership Percentage (%) Saudi Zakat Payable = Max(Saudi Apportioned Base, Saudi Adjusted Net Profit) × [2.5% × (Days in Year ÷ 354)]

*Note: Under standard Gregorian accounting years of 365 days, the statutory calendar conversion factor equates to 2.5% × (365 ÷ 354) = 2.57753% (rounded to 2.578%).

Real-World Case Study: 60/40 Mixed LLC in Riyadh

Consider Al-Najm Logistics LLC, an enterprise established in Riyadh with SAR 10,000,000 in equity and reserves, SAR 2,000,000 in long-term borrowings, and SAR 4,500,000 in net book fixed vehicles and warehouses. The ownership is 60% Saudi partner and 40% German investor.

  • Net Book Income: SAR 2,500,000 with SAR 150,000 unapproved end-of-service provisions and SAR 40,000 book-to-tax depreciation variance. The adjusted net profit totals SAR 2,690,000.
  • Foreign Partner Allocation (40%): Foreign taxable profit equals SAR 2,690,000 × 40% = SAR 1,076,000. Corporate tax payable at 20% is SAR 1,076,000 × 20% = SAR 215,200.
  • Saudi Partner Allocation (60%): Total zakatable capital pool is SAR 12,000,000 - SAR 4,500,000 = SAR 7,500,000. Saudi zakat base share equals SAR 7,500,000 × 60% = SAR 4,500,000.
  • Saudi Zakat Due: For a 365-day Gregorian fiscal year, zakat due is SAR 4,500,000 × 2.57753% = SAR 116,010 (approx. SAR 115,989).
  • Combined Filing Obligation: SAR 215,200 (Tax) + SAR 116,010 (Zakat) = SAR 331,210, resulting in a blended effective tax rate of 12.31% against adjusted profit.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can carried-forward tax losses offset the Saudi Zakat liability?

No. In Saudi tax practice, tax losses can only be carried forward to offset the foreign shareholder's future adjusted taxable profits, capped at 25% of each year's taxable income under Article 21 of the Income Tax Law. For Zakat calculations, prior losses are accounted for through the retained earnings/deficit line in shareholders' equity.

Why is the Gregorian Zakat percentage 2.578% instead of 2.5%?

Islamic Sharia mandates 2.5% based on a complete lunar (Hijri) year of 354 days. When a business keeps books using the Gregorian calendar (365 days), ZATCA prorates the rate mathematically: (2.5% / 354) × 365 = 2.57753%. For leap years of 366 days, the statutory multiplier becomes 2.5847%.

What happens if the calculated Zakat base is negative?

Under ZATCA regulations, if the balance-sheet zakat base is negative or zero, but the company has positive adjusted net profit for the year, Zakat is assessed directly on the Saudi partner's share of adjusted net profit. If both the base and profit are negative, no Zakat is payable for that fiscal year.

Does the 5% Withholding Tax apply when dividends are paid to the foreign partner?

Yes. Corporate income tax of 20% is levied on annual profits earned. When after-tax profits are distributed and repatriated to a non-resident foreign parent or shareholder, a 5% dividend Withholding Tax (WHT) applies under Article 68, unless reduced by an applicable bilateral Double Taxation Treaty (DTT).
