Comprehensive Guide to Saudi Qiwa Contracts, Probation & Notice Periods

Operating in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia requires strict adherence to the Saudi Labor Law and the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development's (MHRSD) unified digital platform, Qiwa. Whether managing fixed-term contracts, navigating probation terminations under Article 53/54, or determining notice periods under Article 75, HR professionals must ensure full alignment to avoid severe penalties and labor disputes.

1. Probation Periods under Saudi Labor Law

Under Article 53 of the Saudi Labor Law, an employee may be placed on a probation period, which must be explicitly stated in the Qiwa unified contract. The probation period cannot exceed 90 days. However, it can be extended in writing up to a maximum of 180 days with mutual consent. During probation, either party may terminate the contract immediately without notice or indemnity, unless the contract specifies otherwise.

2. Notice Periods and Contract Types (Fixed vs. Unfixed)

Following recent labor law amendments, all Saudi employment contracts are formalized electronically through Qiwa as fixed-term contracts (though mapped against standard termination rules). Notice requirements depend on who initiates the termination:

  • Resignation by Employee (Article 75): Requires a minimum of 60 days written notice for contracts renewed automatically or indefinite terms, or 30 days depending on specific contract terms.
  • Termination by Employer: Requires a minimum of 60 days notice unless terminated for gross misconduct under Article 80.

3. Article 77: Arbitrary Termination Compensation

If a contract is terminated without a valid legal reason (arbitrary termination), Article 77 dictates compensation rules:

  • For Fixed-Term Contracts: Wages for the remaining duration of the contract, or a minimum of two months' wages.
  • For Unfixed-Term Contracts: 15 days' wages for each year of service, with a minimum of two months' wages.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Can an employer extend probation beyond 90 days in Qiwa?

A: Yes, but only if agreed upon in writing before the expiration of the initial 90-day period, bringing the total maximum probation to 180 days.

Q: What happens if notice period is not served by the employee?

A: If an employee leaves without serving the mandatory notice period, they may be required to pay compensation equivalent to the wage for the unserved notice period, as governed by Qiwa and contract clauses.

Q: Are Qiwa contracts legally binding in labor courts?

A: Yes, digital contracts attested on the Qiwa platform serve as the primary official evidentiary document in Saudi Labor Courts (Labor Tribunals).
