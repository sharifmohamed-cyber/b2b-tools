Guide to Saudi VAT, Freelancing, and Withholding Tax (WHT)

Operating as a freelancer, independent contractor, or foreign vendor in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia requires strict adherence to regulations set by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA). Understanding how Value Added Tax (VAT) and Withholding Tax (WHT) apply to your invoices is essential for maintaining compliance and avoiding costly penalties.

1. Saudi VAT Rules for Freelancers & Contractors

In Saudi Arabia, mandatory VAT registration is required if your annual taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000. Voluntary registration is available for those with taxable supplies exceeding SAR 187,500. Standard VAT is levied at 15%. However, export of services to entities outside the GCC region may qualify for 0% zero-rating under specific conditions.

2. Understanding Withholding Tax (WHT) on Non-Resident Payments

When a Saudi resident entity makes a payment to a non-resident foreign contractor or service provider, the Saudi entity is legally obligated to deduct WHT at source and remit it to ZATCA. Rates vary depending on the nature of the service: 5% for rent, tickets, and dividends; 15% for management and consulting fees; and up to 20% for technical and professional services.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Do freelance license holders in Saudi Arabia need a VAT certificate?

A: Yes, if your taxable revenue crosses the mandatory threshold of SAR 375,000, or you can register voluntarily if you exceed SAR 187,500 to claim input VAT credits.

Q: Who pays the Withholding Tax, the client or the contractor?

A: The KSA client entity withholds the tax from the contractor's gross invoice amount and pays it directly to ZATCA on behalf of the non-resident contractor.
