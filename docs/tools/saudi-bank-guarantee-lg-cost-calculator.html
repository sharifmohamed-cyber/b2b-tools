Mastering Saudi Bank Guarantees (Letters of Guarantee) for Etimad & Private Tenders

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, participating in public tenders through the Etimad Portal (منصة اعتماد) and securing major engineering, procurement, and contracting (EPC) contracts mandates the issuance of standardized Letters of Guarantee (LG - خطاب الضمان البنكي). Governed by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) and the Saudi Government Tender and Procurement Law (نظام المنافسات والمشتريات الحكومية ولائحته التنفيذية), bank guarantees represent an unconditional, irrevocable commitment by a licensed commercial bank to pay the beneficiary on first demand in the event of contractor default.

Treasury officers, chief financial officers (CFOs), and bid managers often underestimate the compounding financial overhead of these bonds. Between recurring quarterly bank commissions, non-recoverable 15% ZATCA VAT, and the severe liquidity strain of locked cash collateral (الهامش النقدي), an unmanaged guarantee facility can wipe out project margins before ground is broken.

Key Types of Bank Guarantees in Saudi Arabia & Legal Requirements

1. Bid Bond / Tender Guarantee (الضمان الابتدائي)

Mandated by Article 34 of the Saudi Procurement Law to prevent bidders from withdrawing during tender evaluation. Typically 1.0% to 2.0% of estimated bid value. Valid for 90 to 180 days from the bid submission deadline. Upon tender award, unsuccessful bidders receive releases while the winning bidder upgrades to a performance bond.

2. Performance Bond (ضمان حسن التنفيذ / النهائي)

Submitted by the awarded contractor within 10 to 15 business days of notification of award. Usually set at 5.0% of the total contract value for government entities. Must remain active through the completion of initial project handover (الاستلام الابتدائي).

3. Advance Payment Guarantee (ضمان الدفعة المقدمة)

Required before receiving upfront mobilization cash from the project owner (typically 5% to 10% of contract value). The LG face value equals 100% of the advanced amount and amortizes proportionally as monthly progress billings are submitted and approved.

4. Maintenance / Retention Bond (ضمان الصيانة)

Guarantees the rectification of latent defects during the liability period (usually 1 to 5 years after handover). Often replaces the 5% cash retention withheld by the client from interim progress payment certificates.

Mathematical Formulas & Saudi Banking Fee Mechanics

Unlike term loans where interest is calculated daily, Saudi banks bill guarantee issuance commissions strictly on a quarterly basis (3-month block or part thereof). This means an 8-month guarantee is charged as three full quarters (9 months):

# 1. Total Guarantee Face Value (SAR):

LG Face Value = Contract Value × Guarantee Percentage (%)

# 2. Quarterly Tranche Count:

Number of Quarters = Ceiling(Duration in Months ÷ 3)

# 3. Quarterly Bank Commission (SAR):

Quarterly Rate = Annual Commission Rate (%) ÷ 4

Quarterly Fee = MAX(LG Face Value × Quarterly Rate, Minimum Bank Quarterly Tariff)

# 4. Total Commission & VAT:

Total Gross Commission = Quarterly Fee × Number of Quarters

ZATCA 15% VAT = Total Gross Commission × 0.15

# 5. Opportunity Cost of Locked Liquidity (SAR):

Locked Margin = LG Face Value × Cash Collateral Margin (%)

Opportunity Cost = Locked Margin × (Duration in Months ÷ 12) × Cost of Capital (%)

The Hidden Drain: Cash Collateral vs. Corporate Credit Facilities

Emerging contractors or suppliers without established credit lines at major Saudi commercial institutions (such as SNB, Al Rajhi, SAB, Riyad Bank, or Banque Saudi Fransi) are frequently asked to provide 10% to 100% cash collateral (هامش نقدي). This capital is frozen in a non-interest-bearing blocked account for the entire life of the tender or contract.

If an SME locks SAR 500,000 for 18 months at an internal Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) of 8%, the hidden opportunity cost is SAR 60,000. When added to standard bank commissions and non-refundable fees, this cost can exceed the projected profit margin of the project. Contractors must negotiate facility arrangements (تسهيلات غير مباشرة) with banks to lower margin requirements down to 0% to 10%.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - الضمانات البنكية في السعودية

Q1: Can a bank guarantee be extended, and what are the fees?

Yes. In Saudi Arabia, if a project is extended, the client requests a guarantee extension. The bank charges an amendment fee (usually SAR 150 to SAR 400 + 15% VAT) plus the standard quarterly commission rate for each additional quarter added to the validity date.

Q2: How does the release and cancellation process work (فك الضمان)?

The bank cannot release the cash margin or stop charging commissions until it receives the original guarantee letter back from the beneficiary OR an official written release letter from the government authority/client through the Etimad portal or bank-to-bank SWIFT message.

Q3: Is the 15% VAT on bank guarantee commissions reclaimable?

Yes, for VAT-registered commercial entities in Saudi Arabia that make taxable supplies, the 15% VAT charged by banks on bank fees and commissions is eligible for input tax credit deduction on monthly or quarterly ZATCA VAT returns.

Q4: What is the difference between a Letter of Credit (LC) and a Letter of Guarantee (LG)?

An LC (الاعتماد المستندي) is a primary payment mechanism used in international trade to pay a seller upon presentation of complying shipping documents. An LG (خطاب الضمان) is a secondary contingency instrument that is only drawn upon if one party fails to execute contractual duties.
