Comprehensive Guide to Saudi IFRS 16 Commercial Lease Accounting & Ejar Compliance

Under Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants (SOCPA) guidelines, all non-listed entities and public listed companies operating within the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia must account for commercial lease contracts under IFRS 16 (Leases). This standard eliminates the historical off-balance-sheet treatment of operating leases, requiring lessees to recognize almost all commercial leases on their balance sheet through a Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset and a corresponding Lease Liability.

1 Fundamental Accounting Formulas under IFRS 16 / SOCPA

1. Initial Lease Liability (Present Value)

The initial lease liability is measured as the present value of future lease payments discounted using the company’s Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR) or the implicit rate in the lease:

Lease Liability = ∑ [ Payment_t / (1 + r)^t ]

Where r is the periodic discount rate (IBR / Payment Frequency) and t is the specific installment timeline (taking into account payment in advance vs arrears).

2. Right-of-Use (ROU) Initial Cost

The ROU asset starts from the lease liability but factors in all upfront cash transactions:

ROU Asset = Initial Lease Liability + Direct Costs + Restoration PV - Incentives

Includes legal drafting fees, Ejar contract registration fees, agent commissions, and fit-out dismantling provisions.

3. Straight-Line ROU Depreciation

Unless ownership transfers at the end of the term, ROU is depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter of lease duration or useful life:

Periodic Depreciation = Total ROU Asset Value / Total Contract Periods

4. Periodic Finance (Interest) Expense

Computed using the effective interest method on the remaining lease liability balance:

Interest Expense_t = Opening Balance_t × (Annual IBR / Frequency)

2 Interaction with the Saudi Ejar Network & ZATCA VAT

In Saudi Arabia, all commercial leases (warehouses, office buildings, retail showrooms, worker camps) must be registered via the Ministry of Municipal and Rural Affairs & Housing through the Ejar Commercial Electronic Network. For financial controllers and enterprise auditors, the integration creates specific compliance nuances:

  • Tax Invoicing (ZATCA e-Invoicing Phase 2): Commercial real estate rent is generally subject to 15% standard Value Added Tax (VAT). VAT is recognized upon payment or tax invoice issuance, and does not form part of the capitalized IFRS 16 lease liability unless it is non-recoverable input VAT.
  • Electronic Payment Matching: Under the unified Ejar system, installments paid through SADAD or Mada can be automatically cross-referenced against the amortization schedule generated here.
  • Exemptions for Short-Term & Low-Value Leases: Lessees may elect not to apply IFRS 16 recognition to short-term leases (terms of 12 months or less with no purchase option) and leases of low-value assets (e.g., small office IT devices). For those, rent continues to be expensed on a straight-line basis.

3 Current vs. Non-Current Lease Liability Splitting

A frequent audit finding in SOCPA compliance reviews is failing to separate the closing lease liability between Current Liabilities (principal payable within 12 months of the balance sheet date) and Non-Current Liabilities (the remaining principal due after 12 months). 4 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR) should a Saudi SME use?

Under IFRS 16, the lessee uses the rate implicit in the lease if readily determinable. If not, the entity must use its incremental borrowing rate—the interest rate it would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with a similar security, the funds necessary to obtain an asset of similar value. In KSA, finance directors frequently use prevailing SIBOR (Saudi Interbank Offered Rate) + the commercial banking spread quoted on corporate credit lines.

How does IFRS 16 impact corporate EBITDA in Saudi Arabia?

IFRS 16 significantly increases reported EBITDA. Previously, operating lease rent was included in Operating Expenses (OpEx), reducing EBITDA. Under IFRS 16, that rental expense is replaced by Depreciation (ROU asset) and Interest Expense (Lease Liability)—both of which are excluded from EBITDA calculations. However, overall net profit is front-loaded with higher expenses in early contract years.

Are Ejar brokerage and registration fees capitalized into the ROU asset?

Yes. Any direct incremental costs incurred solely to negotiate, execute, or register the lease (such as broker commissions and commercial Ejar notary/documentation fees) must be capitalized directly into the opening Right-of-Use Asset.
