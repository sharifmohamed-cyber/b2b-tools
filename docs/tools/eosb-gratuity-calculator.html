Complete Guide to Saudi End-of-Service Benefits (EOSB) Under Saudi Labor Law

The Saudi End of Service Award (مكافأة نهاية الخدمة), governed by Royal Decree No. M/51 (Saudi Labor Law), is a mandatory statutory severance indemnity due to private sector employees upon the termination of their employment contract. Whether you are an HR manager managing corporate payroll reconciliation, a business owner calculating terminal liabilities under IFRS IAS 19, or an expatriate or Saudi professional verifying your final exit clearance, mastering the formulas under Articles 84, 85, and 87 is vital.

Art. 84

Standard Statutory Accrual

Under Article 84, the worker is entitled to an end-of-service award calculated as:

  • Half a month's wage for each of the first 5 years of service.
  • One full month's wage for each subsequent year beyond 5 years.
  • Fractions of a year are calculated pro-rata according to actual service days.
Art. 85

Employee Resignation Tiers

If the worker resigns voluntarily from an indefinite contract, the statutory award is adjusted by tenure thresholds:

  • Less than 2 years: Zero entitlement (0%).
  • 2 to 5 years: One-third (1/3 or 33.33%) of the statutory award.
  • 5 to 10 years: Two-thirds (2/3 or 66.67%) of the statutory award.
  • 10+ years: Full entitlement (100%).
Art. 87

Exemptions & Full Award

Article 87 establishes scenarios where a resigning employee receives 100% full gratuity regardless of tenure:

  • Female worker resigning within 6 months of marriage.
  • Female worker resigning within 3 months of giving birth.
  • Worker leaving due to force majeure or total permanent disability.

The Accurate Mathematical Formula for Saudi EOSB

Many payroll disputes arise from miscalculating the precise daily wage or applying the wrong wage definition. In Saudi jurisprudence (Article 2 of the Labor Law), the gratuity must be based on the "Actual Wage" (الأجر الفعلي). This encompasses the basic salary plus all regular, recurring allowances—notably fixed housing allowances and fixed transportation stipends.

// STEP 1: Determine Total Monthly Reference Wage (W)
W = Basic_Salary + Housing_Allowance + Transport_Allowance + Recurring_Allowances
// STEP 2: Calculate Exact Service Period in Years (T)
Net_Service_Days = Total_Calendar_Days - Unpaid_Leave_Days
T = Net_Service_Days / 365.25 (or 360/365 per internal policy standards)
// STEP 3: Compute Base Gratuity (Base_EOSB) per Article 84
If T <= 5:
  Base_EOSB = T * (W / 2)
Else (T > 5):
  Base_EOSB = (5 * (W / 2)) + ((T - 5) * W)
// STEP 4: Apply Separation Reduction Ratio (R) per Article 85/87
Final_EOSB = Base_EOSB * R
// STEP 5: Add Terminal Vacation Pay & Deduct Liens
Total_Settlement = Final_EOSB + (Accrued_Leave_Days * (W / 30)) - Deductions

Fixed-Term vs. Indefinite-Term (Unlimited) Contracts

A crucial nuance for non-Saudi employees: Under Article 37 of the Saudi Labor Law, employment contracts for non-Saudis are legally considered fixed-term contracts even if silence exists in the text (defaulting to the validity of the work permit/Iqama). If a fixed-term contract expires naturally and is not renewed by either party, the employee is entitled to 100% full end-of-service gratuity under Article 84, regardless of whether the employer or employee declined renewal.

However, if a fixed-term contract is terminated prematurely by the employee without a legally sanctioned justification under Article 81, Article 85 or contractual damages under Article 77 may apply.

When is Gratuity Completely Forfeited? (Article 80)

Under Article 80 of the Saudi Labor Law, an employer may terminate the contract without notice or indemnity (0% EOSB) under strict conditions, including: assault on the employer or managers, material failure to fulfill basic contractual obligations, proven forgery, misconduct during probation, gross deliberate material loss (reported within 24 hours), unexcused absence exceeding 30 intermittent days or 15 consecutive days (following formal written warnings), or disclosure of enterprise trade secrets.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is end of service calculated on basic salary or total salary? ▼

EOSB is calculated on the last drawn actual wage (الأجر الفعلي), which includes the basic salary plus any regular, fixed allowances such as housing, transportation, and guaranteed commissions. Discretionary bonuses or variable reimbursements are generally excluded unless stipulated as permanent in the contract.

What happens if an employee resigns with 4 years of service? ▼

Under Article 85, a resigning employee with 4 years of service falls into the 2-to-5-year bracket. They are entitled to one-third (1/3) of the standard award. Example: For a salary of SAR 10,000, 4 years standard award is 4 * (10,000/2) = SAR 20,000. One-third entitlement equals SAR 6,666.67.

Can an employer deduct unpaid loans or damages from the gratuity? ▼

Yes. Statutory deductions, outstanding salary advances, equipment liability clearances, or adjudicated damages can be offset against final clearance payments, provided there is signed documentation or legal entitlement.

How quickly must the employer settle the final payment upon separation? ▼

According to Article 88 of the Saudi Labor Law, if the employer terminates the worker, all financial entitlements must be paid within one week. If the worker resigns, the employer must settle all entitlements within two weeks.
