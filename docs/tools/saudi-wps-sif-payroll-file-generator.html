What is the Saudi Wage Protection System (WPS)?

          

            The Wage Protection System (نظام حماية الأجور) is a mandatory nationwide regulatory program initiated by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) in partnership with the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA). The mandate compels all private sector entities registered in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia to disburse employee compensation directly into authorized Saudi bank accounts using the standardized SIF (Salary Interchange Format / Standard Interchange File) layout.
          

          

            Managed predominantly through the unified Mudad (مُدد) platform, the framework monitors that each worker receives their agreed compensation without unjustified deductions or systemic delays. Failure to maintain an 80% or greater compliance rating over consecutive billing cycles triggers immediate operational penalties, including suspension of Qiwa work permit renewals and company licensing restrictions.
          

        


        

          
SIF File Anatomy & Record Technical Structure

          

            A valid Saudi SIF file is an ASCII text document comprising strict comma-delimited columns without extra formatting, special characters, or trailing spaces. The architecture consists of two primary tiers:
          

          
    
            
  • File Header Record (SCR): Identifies the corporate identity. It features the Establishment MOL Number (often the 700 identifier), the corporate English name, the file creation timestamp, the client corporate clearing bank identifier (e.g., RJHI, NCBK, RIBL), and unique file reference sequences.
    • 
            
  • Employee Salary Records (EDR): Each line holds the employee's 10-digit National ID or Iqama number, their unique International Bank Account Number (IBAN starting with SA), Basic Salary, Housing Allowance, Other Earnings, Deductions, and the designated Employee Bank Code.
    •

The Top 5 Causes of Saudi WPS File Rejection & How to Prevent Them

        
    
          
  1. IBAN / Bank Code Inconsistency: A frequent rejection occurs when the 2-letter or 4-letter bank identifier in the IBAN characters 5 to 6 does not match the designated receiving bank routing destination code in the corporate online banking wizard.
    2. 
          
  2. Deductions Exceeding Statutory Thresholds: Under Article 92 of the Saudi Labor Law, employer deductions (excluding court-ordered alimony or debt) must never surpass 50% of an employee's total gross remuneration without prior written administrative approval.
    3. 
          
  3. Unregistered Establishment IDs: Inserting an obsolete municipal commercial license or incomplete 700 series entity number leads to immediate batch decline at the SAMA clearing exchange switch.
    4. 
          
  4. Invalid National ID or Expired Iqama Formatting: Failure to provide exactly 10 digits or including hyphenated spaces causes core banking parsers to abort file ingestion.
    5. 
          
  5. Net Salary Mismatches against Wage Protection Contracts: If the basic wage transmitted in the SIF differs drastically from the authenticated contract recorded on the Qiwa platform without an uploaded justification file, Mudad flags the variance as a violation.

Frequently Asked Questions (WPS & Mudad FAQ)

        

          

            
              Can an employee be paid in cash under Saudi Labor Regulations?
              ▼
            
            

              No. Under the comprehensive Wage Protection System regulations, cash payment is prohibited for private sector commercial entities. All wages must be disbursed through approved bank channels via SIF files or the Mudad digital payroll gateway to achieve recognized compliance.
            

          


          

            
              What is the difference between Mudad Payroll and Bank Direct SIF Upload?
              ▼
            
            

              Mudad operates directly as the official compliance clearinghouse for MHRSD. While companies can either initiate salaries straight through Mudad or upload the SIF file via their corporate bank portal (e.g., Al Rajhi Business, SNB e-Corporate), both systems share identical SIF data schemas and file validation engines.
            

          


          

            
              What happens if our company misses the monthly payroll deadline?
              ▼
            
            

              If salaries are delayed past the scheduled payment date (ordinarily within 10 days of the month end), Mudad issues automated compliance warnings. A two-month delay blocks work transfer permissions, while a three-month delay permits affected employees to legally transfer their sponsorship without employer consent.
