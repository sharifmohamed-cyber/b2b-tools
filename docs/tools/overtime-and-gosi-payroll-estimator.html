Comprehensive Guide to Overtime & GOSI Payroll Management in GCC SMEs

Managing payroll accurately is one of the most critical responsibilities for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operating in the GCC. Compliance with regional labor laws—such as the Saudi Labor Law or UAE Labor Law—requires precise calculation of working hours, overtime compensation, allowances, and social insurance deductions like GOSI (General Organization for Social Insurance).

1. How Overtime is Calculated Under Labor Laws

Under standard regional regulations, normal working hours are typically capped at 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week. Any hours worked beyond this threshold must be compensated at a higher rate. The standard overtime hourly rate is calculated based on the employee's basic salary plus mandatory allowances (depending on local statutory interpretations, though frequently calculated against basic wage). The multiplier is usually set at a minimum of 1.5 times the hourly rate for standard overtime work, and up to 2.0 times for work performed on weekly rest days or official public holidays.

2. Understanding GOSI Contributions

The General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) administers social insurance schemes that provide retirement pensions, occupational hazard protection, and unemployment insurance (SANED). For GCC nationals, the standard contribution rate is typically split between the employer (approx. 12% for annuities + 2% for occupational hazards) and the employee (approx. 9% to 10% for annuities). Expatriate workers are generally only subject to the occupational hazard branch (typically 2% paid entirely by the employer). Ensuring correct wage caps and contribution brackets prevents compliance penalties and audit risks.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Are housing and transport allowances included in GOSI wage calculations?

A: In many GCC frameworks (such as Saudi Arabia), GOSI contributions are calculated based on the monthly basic wage plus housing allowance (up to specific regulatory caps or housing provided in-kind), whereas other allowances may be excluded. Always check current statutory updates.

Q: Can an employee agree to waive overtime compensation in lieu of extra days off?

A: In most jurisdictions, labor laws strictly mandate monetary compensation for overtime hours unless specific mutual written agreements comply with local labor ministry executive regulations regarding time-in-lieu.
