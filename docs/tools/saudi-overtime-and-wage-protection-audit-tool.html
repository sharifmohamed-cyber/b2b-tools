Complete Guide to Saudi Overtime (Article 107) & WPS Compliance Guide

Operating a business in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia requires strict adherence to the Saudi Labor Law and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) regulations. Two critical pillars of local HR administration are accurate overtime calculation under Article 107 and seamless Wage Protection System (WPS) / Mudad compliance.

1. How Article 107 Overtime is Calculated

According to Saudi Labor Law Article 107, if an employer requires an employee to work additional hours beyond normal working hours, compensation must be paid at a rate not less than 150% (1.5x) of the normal hourly wage. Furthermore, work performed on weekends (Rest Days) or official public holidays must be compensated at 200% (2.0x). The hourly wage is typically calculated based on the basic salary plus contractual allowances divided by normal monthly working hours.

2. Wage Protection System (WPS) & Mudad Integration

The WPS mandates that all private sector employers transfer employee wages through approved financial institutions and report them via the Mudad platform. Discrepancies between the GOSI registered wage and actual WPS transfers can trigger Mudad compliance flags, leading to suspension of government services or fines. Regular internal audits of overtime payouts ensure transparency and compliance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Is overtime mandatory for all employees?

A: Managerial and high-level executive employees are often exempt from overtime compensation under specific contract clauses, whereas operational and administrative staff are generally eligible.

Q: How does Mudad check wage compliance?

A: Mudad cross-references monthly bank transfer files with employment contracts registered on Qiwa and GOSI wage records to detect unpaid or delayed salaries.
