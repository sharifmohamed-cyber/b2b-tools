Comprehensive Guide to ZATCA VAT Bad Debt Relief in Saudi Arabia (Article 40)

In Saudi Arabia, registered corporate taxpayers frequently confront delinquent commercial accounts receivables. When a buyer defaults on an unpaid tax invoice, the seller who previously remitted the 15% (or 5%) Value Added Tax (VAT) out of pocket to the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) effectively bears an unfair cash shortfall. To mitigate this commercial burden, the Saudi VAT Implementing Regulations provide a statutory mechanism under Article 40 (Bad Debts Relief / الديون المعدومة) enabling registered taxpayers to reduce output tax previously reported on uncollectible debts. Understanding the stringent prerequisites is vital to avoid harsh tax penalties and disallowed deductions during ZATCA tax inspections.

Mathematical Formulas for Bad Debt Tax Write-Back

VAT components are isolated using tax-fraction mathematics derived from the gross outstanding balance:

Net Unpaid Outstanding = Total Gross Invoice - Collections Received

Tax Fraction (15%) = 15 ÷ 115 ≈ 0.1304348

Recoverable Output Tax Relief = Net Unpaid Outstanding × [R ÷ (1 + R)]

Net Bad Debt Loss (P&L Write-Off) = Net Unpaid Outstanding - Recoverable Output Tax

ZATCA Return Reporting Mechanism (Box 1)

Approved bad debt VAT relief must not be booked arbitrarily as an input tax expense. Instead, under ZATCA guidelines:

  • It is declared in Box 1 (Standard Rated Sales) under the "Adjustments" (التعديلات) column.
  • The entry is recorded as a negative adjustment value, directly lowering the overall taxable sales and the net output tax payable.
  • If the customer later settles all or part of the bad debt, the supplier must reverse the adjustment in Box 1 within the tax period of recovery.

Five Mandatory Preconditions Under ZATCA Article 40

ZATCA auditors examine bad debt adjustments meticulously. Taxpayers cannot deduct output VAT unless they verify and archive proofs for five sequential conditions:

  1. The Default Period of 12 Months: Exactly 12 months must have elapsed from either the invoice date or the explicit agreed payment due date stated in the commercial contract, whichever occurs later. If standard trade credit was 60 days, the 12-month timeline begins after the 60-day default date.
  2. Prior Tax Declaration & Payment: The seller must hold proof that output tax on the original supply was declared in a previous ZATCA VAT return and actually remitted through SADAD.
  3. Written Notification to Customer: The supplier must deliver a formal written notice to the customer stating the exact amount written off and informing the customer that they are legally obliged under Article 40(4) to make an equal downward adjustment to their own input tax previously deducted.
  4. Exhaustion of Commercial Recovery Procedures: The business must substantiate that customary commercial collection efforts failed. Acceptable proofs include documented legal cases via the Saudi Ministry of Justice (Najiz), commercial court execution orders, proof of debtor's insolvency or bankruptcy under the Saudi Bankruptcy Law (نظام الإفلاس), or evidence from collection agencies.
  5. Accounting Write-Off Certified by an SOCPA Auditor: The uncollectible sum must be formally charged off to the company's profit and loss account (Allowance for Doubtful Debts or Bad Debt Expense) and certified via an annual statutory audit letter issued by a licensed SOCPA practitioner.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if the customer pays after the debt was written off and VAT was relieved?

Under Article 40, Paragraph 5, if the customer subsequently pays the full balance or an installment, the supplier must treat the collected proportion as output tax. The corresponding VAT must be re-added as a positive adjustment in Box 1 of the VAT return during the period the money was deposited.

Can bad debt relief be claimed on invoices issued to government entities in KSA?

Generally, receivables from Saudi government ministries and public authorities are considered sovereign debts and are not eligible for bad debt relief under standard default provisions, as government payment obligations do not legally lapse into formal bankruptcy.

What is the customer's legal obligation when receiving a bad debt notice?

When a registered customer receives written notification that their supplier has written off an overdue invoice, the customer is legally required to make a negative adjustment to their Input Tax in Box 7 of their ZATCA VAT return, effectively repaying the input tax credit originally claimed on the unpaid goods or services.

Does ZATCA require a specific form or certificate for bad debts?

ZATCA does not issue a standalone pre-printed clearance certificate. Instead, taxpayers must maintain a dedicated audit trail binder comprising the original tax invoice, proof of tax return submission, bank statements showing non-receipt, copies of registered notification letters sent to the debtor, court enforcement filing logs (Najiz), and the licensed external auditor’s verification memo.
